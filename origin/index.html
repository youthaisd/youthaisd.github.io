ORIGIN / CONTEXT NOTE

Where the question began.

A workshop and a youth discussion paper in Shanghai prompted a question about what might continue after an event ends. YouthAISD is an independent response to that question.

SHANGHAI · 5 JULY 2026

Inspiration is a historical connection, not an institutional mandate.

01 / THE EVENT

Shanghai, July 2026.

On 5 July 2026, the Youth Day workshop Zero Waste in an AI-Powered Future took place in Shanghai. It brought young participants together to explore AI, zero waste and the circular economy.

The workshop was organized by the UNEP International Environmental Technology Centre (UNEP IETC), the Basel Convention Regional Centre for Asia and the Pacific (BCRC China), and the Children and Youth Major Group to UNEP (CYMG). This is a description of the event, not of YouthAISD’s institutional relationships.

02 / THE PAPER

From a workshop to a discussion paper.

The Shanghai Youth Discussion Paper on Zero Waste and AI set out a collective youth vision and calls to action under the longer subtitle Harnessing Artificial Intelligence to Accelerate the Transition to Zero Waste and a Circular Economy: Empowering Youth for a Sustainable Future.

Its text considers possible AI applications in circular systems alongside AI’s environmental costs, unequal access, misleading outputs, privacy, environmental justice, youth participation and cross-border knowledge sharing. It is more specific than a general claim that AI will automatically advance sustainability.

03 / THE QUESTIONS THAT REMAINED

Two commitments, two questions.

In “Our Commitments as Children and Youth,” points (c) and (d) look beyond discussion. YouthAISD takes them as prompts for its own questions, not as instructions it was appointed to carry out.

COMMITMENT (C)

From discussion to practical work.

“Developing and scaling youth-led AI solutions for zero waste…”

The useful challenge is whether a discussion can lead, where appropriate, to concrete experimentation and work. That does not mean every problem needs an AI solution, or that YouthAISD has already built one.

05 / SCOPE

Why extend beyond zero waste?

Zero waste was the context in which the question emerged, not a rule for every future project. The broader question—how young people can engage critically and substantively with AI in sustainable development—also arises in health, education, environmental systems, cities and other fields.

YouthAISD uses the Sustainable Development Goals as a framework for locating questions. Work should follow a specific problem, relevant evidence and a credible reason to contribute.

Shanghai is where the question began—not where its boundaries end.

06 / INDEPENDENCE

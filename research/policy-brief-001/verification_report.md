# Verification report — YouthAISD Policy Brief 001

**Review cut-off and access date:** 9 October 2026  
**Document status:** Draft v0.1; local files only  
**Scope:** English-first review of selected official Hong Kong policy documents from 2025–2026; the 2022 Youth Development Blueprint was used as background. UN SDG target descriptions support YouthAISD's analytical mappings.

- *The 2026–27 Budget Speech*, delivered 25 February 2026.
- *The Chief Executive's 2026 Policy Address*, issued 16 September 2026.

- United Nations Sustainable Development Goals target pages for 4.4, 9.5, 13.2, 16.6, and 16.7.

The policy documents and release dates were checked against official English-language government pages, PDFs, or release records. Official originals and section-specific URLs are listed in sources.md; the evidence matrix records paragraph/page locations and verification status.

The matrix contains **17 policy evidence records**. Every E01–E17 record is referenced in the brief. Short quotations are checked against the cited official English source. The UN references support target definitions only; SDG mappings are YouthAISD's analysis, not government classifications.

## Factual audit

- The 2025 Policy Address's HK$2 billion is described as set aside under the Quality Education Fund for digital education, not as verified spending or outcomes.
- The Blueprint's minimum of 30 digital-education training hours applies within each teacher's three-year CPD cycle. The annual 50,000 figure is a training-place target, not a verified number trained.
- The Budget's HK$50 million is an announced commitment. Its 27 undergraduate programmes are STEAM-related in total over the 2025/26–2027/28 triennium, including AI; they are not 27 AI-only programmes.
- The 2026 Policy Address's more-than-200 AI courses and activities by Q1 2028 is a future target.
- The Chinese Academy of Sciences internships are described as high-end scientific-research placements, not AI-specific placements. The young-researcher category is announced without eligibility or award outcomes in the cited paragraph.
- Talent Foundry's around-500 figure is expected annual training opportunities, not completed participants.
- The seven AI risk-governance dimensions and bidder safety/ethics declaration pilot are attributed to the 2026 Policy Address. Wider extension of the pilot is conditional on results.
- The Digital Policy Office guideline is treated as published guidance; no compliance or impact is inferred.
- The Five-Year Plan's electricity targets are kept separate from AI measures: cease coal use for daily electricity generation by 2035 and raise zero-carbon energy to about 60–70 per cent.
- No student-learning, spending, take-up, compliance, or social/environmental outcomes are claimed.

## Interpretation audit

The brief distinguishes:

1. **Government policy facts:** what an official document states and whether it is a direction, released framework, funding commitment, target, or pilot.
2. **YouthAISD interpretation:** education and training pathways are comparatively specified; research and innovation pathways are also named; the cited AI-governance clauses do not specify a standing youth role in design, governance, or evaluation.
3. **YouthAISD proposals:** map available opportunities, assess one bounded public-facing AI use case using public information and safeguards, and send findings to the responsible programme owner.

The youth-governance observation is bounded to the selected document set and search period. A broader youth exchange measure on public affairs and science is acknowledged as civic context; it is not recast as an AI-governance mechanism. Not finding a standing mechanism in these clauses is not proof that none exists elsewhere.

The SDG mappings are explicitly attributed to YouthAISD: 4.4 for skills, 9.5 for research capacity, 16.6 and 16.7 as analytical lenses for governance and participation, and 13.2 for climate planning. Zero-carbon energy is not mapped directly to renewable-energy target 7.2.

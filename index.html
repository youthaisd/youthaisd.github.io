01 / Why this exists

The missing connections matter.

AI and sustainable development are often discussed in the same sentence. The harder work is connecting questions, people, and evidence in ways that can withstand scrutiny.

01

From possibility to problem

Technology conversations can begin with a tool before understanding the local need it might serve.

02

Across fields

Technical, social, and environmental knowledge often sit apart, even when the question requires all three.

03

From voice to contribution

Young people are often invited to speak, but less often supported to shape a question or produce substantive work.

04

From claim to evidence

Promising ideas need clear limits, critical review, and attention to who might benefit or bear the costs.

02 / Origin

A question carried forward from Shanghai.

The initiative was inspired by youth conversations in Shanghai around zero waste and the distance between discussion and sustained action.

That experience informs the starting point, not the boundaries of every future research topic.

03 / Scope

The SDGs are a framework, not a checklist.

The Sustainable Development Goals help locate meaningful questions and see their connections. They are not a promise to cover all 17 goals or to treat every use of AI as progress.

Work should begin with a specific problem, consider the people and context around it, and ask whether an AI approach is appropriate at all.

04 / Approach

A working method, not a record of achievements.

These four verbs describe how a question may move from inquiry toward useful action. Each step depends on what the evidence and contributors make possible.

  1. 01

    Explore

    Listen, map the problem, and test assumptions before deciding what to investigate.

  2. 02

    Publish

    Share findings with transparent methods, clear limits, and appropriate credit.

  3. 03

    Convene

    Where useful, bring contributors and practitioners together around specific questions.

  4. 04

    Build

    Explore small, responsible applications only when a real need and a credible path emerge.

05 / Current work

Three participation routes · open

We start by listening.

The first consultation is designed to learn how young researchers and practitioners see the opportunities, limits, and unanswered questions at the intersection of AI and sustainable development.

The consultation is open for voluntary responses from adults aged 18 or older. Share your perspective to help shape an initial research agenda.

Open the consultation ↗

Questions to open with

  1. 01

    Which problems merit closer study, and whose knowledge is missing?

  2. 02

    Where might AI help, and where might it distract from better solutions?

  3. 03

    What evidence would make a proposed contribution credible?

These are starting prompts, not predetermined priorities.

06 / Participation

Participation follows the work.

There is no membership application in this release. Participation begins by sharing a perspective, mapping a project, or offering a first contribution. A submitted first contribution is reviewed before Contributor status is confirmed.

Core team responsibilities require sustained work. A Contributor does not represent the initiative. Contributions may take the form of research, careful review, field insight, writing, or practical experimentation, with appropriate credit for actual work.

See public acknowledgements ↗

Principles for the work

  • 01
    Intellectual honesty

    Say what is known, what remains uncertain, and what has not been done.

  • 02
    Context before technology

    Begin with people, institutions, and place rather than a preferred tool.

  • 03
    Open but accountable

    Welcome different disciplines while making methods and responsibilities clear.

  • 04
    Useful scale

    Favor focused, verifiable work over claims of reach or impact.
